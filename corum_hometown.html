About Çorum
Çorum is a city in northern Central Anatolia, Türkiye. With a population of around 230,000, it is a calm, safe, and welcoming place to live. The city is widely known as the "Leblebi Capital" of the world — leblebi are a type of roasted chickpea beloved all across Türkiye. Çorum blends a deep historical background with a close-knit modern community.
Extra Info
Çorum has a continental climate with cold, snowy winters and hot, dry summers. The city is surrounded by rolling steppe hills. Ankara is roughly 230 km to the southwest, and Samsun on the Black Sea coast is about 180 km to the north. Despite its modest size, Çorum attracts visitors from all over the world thanks to the nearby UNESCO site of Hattusa.
Interesting Facts
  • Çorum's leblebi is exported to dozens of countries worldwide.
  • Hattusa, the Hittite Empire's capital, is just 85 km away — a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1986.
  • The annual Hitit Kültür Festivali brings thousands of tourists every summer.
  • Çorum ranks among the safest and most peaceful cities in Türkiye.
  • The city's pedestrian boulevard is one of the longest in Central Anatolia.
Cuisine
The undisputed star is leblebi — roasted chickpeas in salted, spicy, or plain varieties. Locals also enjoy keşkek, a slow-cooked wheat and lamb stew made at weddings; Çorum mantısı, tiny dumplings with garlicky yoghurt; and hearty lamb soups. For dessert, höşmerim — a warm, sweet cheese dish — is a traditional favourite.
History
Çorum has a recorded history of over 4,000 years. The Hittite Empire, one of the ancient world's great civilisations, made this region its heartland around 1600 BCE. Their capital, Hattusa, near today's Boğazkale, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. After the Hittites, the region passed through Phrygian, Persian, Roman, Byzantine, and Seljuk hands before joining the Ottoman Empire in the 15th century.
